Interactive Installation / Collective Voice

Blood Is
Not a Sin

The blood was never the problem. The silence was. A monumental body built from sanitary pads receives messages about bodily autonomy, freedom, and dignity from around the world.

Taoyuan Design Exhibition Finalist
TypeInteractive installation · Public issue
Scale180 × 180 × 160 cm
MaterialSanitary pads · Structure · Projection
ExperienceMessage · Translation · Projection · Voice wall
The question

Why is a natural sign of life treated as an error that must not be seen?

Layered sanitary pads cover a woman’s face and body, leaving only her eyes to meet the viewer. The form behaves at once as veil, armor, and accumulated pressure, enlarging hidden experiences of menstruation, bodily autonomy, and women’s voices to an inescapable scale.

01 / Object

Stigma becomes a body that cannot be ignored.

The soft white surface carries an expectation of cleanliness. Red stains interrupt it. Four views show the work as a presence that occupies space rather than a single frontal image.

02 / Experience

Every message replaces a stigma.

A visitor leaves a message on a phone. The system translates it and projects it onto the sanitary-pad surface. Voices in different languages cross borders and turn a field of wounds into one of support and connection.

01

Write

Leave a message on a phone or tablet.

02

Translate

The message is translated into 50+ languages.

03

Project

The words appear on the installation.

04

Gather

Each voice joins a global wall of testimony.

03 / The Voice Beneath

The voices continue beyond the installation.

The mobile platform lets participants read messages, share their own, and see a multilingual community mapped across the world.

04 / In Space

This is not an object asking for quiet admiration.

Visitors walk around it, meet its gaze, and add their own words. The work becomes complete through participation.
